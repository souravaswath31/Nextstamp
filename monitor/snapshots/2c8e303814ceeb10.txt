Crime levels in Mauritius are low.
However, petty crime and opportunistic theft occurs, including pickpocketing and bag snatching.
This is most likely to occur in crowded outdoor shopping areas, tourist areas, such as beaches, and near ATMs.
There have also been cases of theft at unregistered beachside bungalows.
The crime rate is higher in downtown Port Louis and in the coastal tourist centres of Grand Bay, Pereybere and Flic en Flac.
Women walking alone are at greater risk of verbal harassment and criminal targeting.
Violent crime is not common, but incidents of  assault ,  rape and murder have occurred, including in resorts.
To protect yourself from crime:
be aware of your surroundings and alert to suspicious behaviour
avoid secluded or poorly lit areas, especially after dark
avoid using ATMs on the street - use ATMS in banks, shops, hotels or shopping centres
always lock your vehicle and accommodation and keep your valuables out of sight
stay in accommodation registered with the Mauritius Tourism Authority .
If you are a victim of crime, contract the Mauritius tourism police on 999.
Tourism police (Mauritius Police Department)

check the credentials of the driver and agree on the fare, before entering the vehicle
where possible, book taxis through your hotel or accommodation.

In Mauritius, legal cases take a long time to process.
If you're under police investigation, you'll be provisionally charged and won't be allowed to leave the country without consent from a judge.
It can take up to 2 years for formal charges to be laid.
You're not allowed to renew your occupation permit or resident permit if you’re provisionally charged with a crime.
If you cannot support yourself financially, you’ll be kept in prison while the police finish their investigation.
Mauritius has severe penalties for drug offences, including fines and jail terms of up to 60 years.
Pack all luggage yourself and do not carry anything through customs for anyone else.
If you're found with any drugs, it’s unlikely you’ll be bailed, regardless of the type of drug.
Prosecutions can take more than a year before going to court.
You’ll be detained until your trial.

Ebola border measures
The Government of Mauritius has introduced border measures in response to the Ebola disease outbreak in parts of Africa.
These include restrictions on entry and health screening checks for travellers who have recently visited DRC, Uganda or South Sudan:
Non-Mauritian nationals that have travelled from DRC, Uganda or South Sudan within the preceding 21 days and that do not hold a valid work permit, residence permit, occupation permit, business, or student visa are not allowed to enter Mauritius
Mauritian nationals, and non-Mauritians holding a valid work permit, residence permit, occupation permit, business visa or student visa who have travelled from, transited through, or been present in DRC, Uganda or South Sudan within the preceding 21 days will be permitted entry but will be subject to a 21-day mandatory quarantine.
Contact the Ministry of Health and Wellness Hotline on +230 8924 in Mauritius for further information.
The Mauritian Ministry of Health and Wellness advises Mauritian citizens and residents, which may include Australian citizens and dual nationals, to avoid non-essential travel to DRC and South Sudan.
All travellers entering Mauritius are subject to the public health measures and entry requirements in force at the time of their arrival.
Border measures can change at short notice and you must cooperate with health authorities.
Check the latest requirements with your airline or travel provider before travelling to Mauritius.

You should complete the  Mauritius All-in-One Travel Digital Form   either online before you travel or when you arrive at the airport.
If you're arriving from a country known to have malaria, you'll receive a follow-up call or visit from the Mauritian Health Department.
