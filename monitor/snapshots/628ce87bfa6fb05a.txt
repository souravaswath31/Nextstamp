ENTRY AND VISAS Is a visa required to enter Uzbekistan?
Citizens of most countries may enter Uzbekistan visa-free or benefit from simplified visa procedures.
Uzbekistan has established a visa-free regime with more than 90 countries (see below).
Nationals of these states are permitted to stay in the Uzbekistan without a visa, generally for up to 30 days, and in certain cases up to 60 days, depending on the applicable bilateral agreement.
For citizens of countries not included on the visa-free list, an electronic visa (e-Visa) is available through the official portal e-visa.gov.uz, eliminating the need to apply in person at a consular office.
Entry into the Uzbekistan requires a valid international passport with at least six months’ validity remaining from the date of entry.
Foreign nationals must register their place of stay within three working days of arrival; when staying at a hotel, this procedure is carried out automatically by the hotel administration.
Failure to comply with registration requirements entails administrative liability.

What is an electronic visa?
The electronic visa of the Uzbekistan (e-Visa) is an official entry authorisation issued in electronic format through the state portal e-visa.gov.uz, without the need to apply in person at a consular or diplomatic mission.
This document entitles the holder to a single-entry stay in Uzbekistan for tourism, business, or transit purposes for up to 30 days, and in certain cases up to 60 days, depending on the applicable bilateral agreement.
From a legal standpoint, the e-Visa is a strictly controlled document equivalent to a passport visa stamp and is recognised by all border control authorities of the Uzbekistan.
To apply, a valid international passport, a digital photograph, and payment of the consular fee are required.
The processing time is generally 2 to 5 working days.
REGISTRATION AND STAY Is a foreign national staying in Uzbekistan required to register their place of residence?
In accordance with the Procedure for Entry into and Exit from the Uzbekistan for Foreign Nationals and Stateless Persons (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No.
408 of 21 November 1996), foreign nationals and stateless persons are required to register their place of stay within three working days of entering the territory of the Uzbekistan.
Registration is carried out through the Unified Electronic Registration and Identification System on the portal my.gov.uz.
When staying at hotels, sanatoria, rest houses, or other organisations providing temporary accommodation, the obligation to register the foreign national rests with the administration of the respective establishment.
When staying with private individuals, registration is carried out by the host party through the same portal or by contacting the relevant territorial internal affairs authority.
Failure to fulfil the registration obligation entails administrative liability under the applicable legislation of the Uzbekistan.
The Procedure, however, provides for a number of exceptions: in particular, foreign nationals transiting through the Uzbekistan or staying for a period not exceeding three working days are exempt from the mandatory registration requirement.
Upon a change of place of stay, the foreign national is required to re-register at the new address within the same three-day period.
The registration confirmation document must be kept on one’s person throughout the entire period of stay in the country and produced upon request by the competent authorities, including at exit border control.
What liability applies to foreign nationals staying in Uzbekistan for failure to register their place of residence?
In accordance with the Procedure for Entry into and Exit from the Uzbekistan for Foreign Nationals and Stateless Persons (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No.
408 of 21 November 1996) and the Code of the Uzbekistan on Administrative Liability, a foreign national or stateless person who violates the established registration requirements at their place of stay shall be subject to administrative liability in the form of a warning or a fine.
Administrative liability attaches both to the foreign national themselves and to the hosting party — whether an individual or a legal entity — that failed to fulfil its obligation to register the visitor in a timely manner.
Cases involving these administrative offences fall within the jurisdiction of the internal affairs authorities of the Uzbekistan.
In addition to an administrative fine, the aforementioned Procedure provides for more serious legal consequences in cases of systematic or egregious violations of immigration legislation.
In particular, the competent authorities may decide to curtail the period of permitted stay of the foreign national in the Uzbekistan, and in cases that pose a threat to public order or national security, may initiate compulsory expulsion (deportation) followed by a prohibition on re-entry.
It should be particularly noted that a registration violation may be detected at exit border control, which may result in the foreign national being detained and an administrative report being drawn up at the point of entry or exit.
Accordingly, strict compliance with registration requirements is a mandatory condition for lawful stay in the Uzbekistan.
Using e-Government Services Can foreign citizens use electronic government services?
Foreign citizens can use selected e-government services after completing the required identification and registration procedures.
What do I need to access online public services?
You need a valid passport, a PINFL, and registration in the OneID system.
Documents & Identification What is JShShIR and why it is required?
A Personal Identification Number (JShShIR) is issued to: citizens of the Republic of Uzbekistan; foreign citizens and stateless persons residing in Uzbekistan on a temporary or permanent basis; foreign citizens and stateless persons permanently residing abroad; and foreign citizens temporarily staying or operating in the Republic of Uzbekistan through an authorized representative.
The issuance of a Personal Identification Number is free of charge.
JShShIR is required for public services and official procedures.
You can obtain a PINFL at Public Service Centers or migration offices.
Payments & Fees Can I pay for government services online?
Payment for services on the my.gov.uz portal is made online through the personal account using bank cards (UzCard, Humo), payment applications (Uzum Bank), as well as in installments.
To make a payment, you need to select a service, enter the invoice number, verify the amount, and confirm the transaction, receiving a receipt with a QR code.
Main payment methods: In most cases, fees are non-refundable.
What are the main payment methods on my.gov.uz?
Bank cards: UzCard, Humo, linked in your personal account.
Payment systems: Payme, Click, Uzum Bank.
Installment plans: Uzum Nasiya for paying for government services.
How do I pay for a service on my.gov.uz?
When submitting your application, select a payment method.
Enter the invoice number and amount, if not filled in automatically.
Verify the details (full name, payment purpose).
Pay by card or choose the installment option.
Save or download the electronic receipt with a QR code.
What are the advantages of paying online on my.gov.uz?
Commission-free payment (in most cases).
Automatic registration of the payment in the system, which speeds up application processing.
Availability of payment for fines, taxes, utility bills, and state duties.
Common Issues & Support Can I correct a mistake in my application?
Some applications allow corrections before submission.
Where can I get help?
Support is available via the portal hotline or feedback form.
