Kidnapping is a serious risk in Mexico.
Kidnapping occurs throughout the country, with the highest number of cases recorded along the Gulf Coast from Tamaulipas to Tabasco states, and in large cities including Mexico City.
Drug cartels, criminal and terrorist organisations have all been involved in kidnapping.
Kidnappers target a broad demographic, including wealthy Mexicans, migrants seeking to cross the US border, as well as foreigners and expatriates.
Motives are also varied, with political kidnappings and forced disappearance reported across the country.
Victims of traditional kidnappings are physically abducted and held captive until a ransom is paid.

Some victims claim police officers or other officials are involved in their kidnapping.
Virtual or scam kidnappings target people over the phone or online to extort money.
Kidnappers pose as officials or cartel members and demand payments for the release of a family member they have allegedly detained.
Criminal groups may also attempt to extort relatives of tourists reported as missing in news reports.
Foreign nationals have been kidnapped and/or assaulted by people they have met through online dating apps, with victims and their families extorted for large sums of money to secure their release.
Express kidnappings are also regularly reported.
Kidnappers may hold victims in unauthorised taxis, then force victims to withdraw funds from ATMs, before being released.

Visas and border measures
Each country or territory decides its own entry and exit rules, including health border screening measures.
To find out what you need to enter a destination, ask their embassy, consulate or immigration office.
If you have an ordinary passport with a chip, and you're visiting for tourism, you can use the e-gates available at a number of major airports.
This authorises Australian citizens to stay for up to 180 days only for tourism purposes.
You'll then receive a printed QR code where you can check the number of days you're authorised to stay in Mexico.
You'll also receive a receipt and have 60 days to download your e-tourist card.
To use the e-gates you must:
have a valid ordinary passport with a chip
be at least 18 years old
enter the country only for tourism purposes and not for business, study, or other purposes
not be traveling with minors
You can't use the e-gates if you have a valid visa and/or immigration document issued by the Mexican Government, such as:
permanent resident visa/card
temporary resident visa/card
student resident visa/card
visitor visa/card for humanitarian reasons
visitor visa/card for adoption purposes.
If you don't have an ordinary passport with a chip and you visit for up to 180 days or less as a tourist, you can get a visa on arrival.
Be aware of the date that's stamped in your passport on arrival, as you may not receive the full 180 days to stay.
Your visa will expire on a date given by the immigration officer on arrival.
This is usually for the amount of time you indicate to the immigration officer that you're staying in Mexico.
This 180-day period isn't guaranteed, and the length of stay is up to immigration authorities.
To avoid being detained or deported, you'll need to:
fill in an online Multiple Immigration Form (FMM) and obtain a QR code
make sure your passport is stamped by immigration officials on arrival
You can complete the FMM online before you arrive or on arrival.
If you're entering by road, make sure you get the stamp on your passport at the immigration office  (Instituto Nacional de Migración: Spanish) .
These are usually located near, but not directly at, a border crossing.
Present your completed FMM for inspection at immigration if entering by air.
You'll need to show it when you leave Mexico.
To cross the land border between Mexico and the US, you'll need to verbally provide the reason for your travel.
If you're entering from the United States, and no immigration officer is available to stamp your passport, locate the nearest immigration office before continuing your travel.
These offices are typically close to the border, and customs officials can guide you.
It’s usually more difficult to resolve immigration issues after leaving the border area.
Confirm your travel and transit arrangements directly with your airline or travel agent.
If you enter by land, your length of stay will be determined on arrival by immigration authorities, and could be up to 180 days.
You'll be required to pay a fee.
Non-compliance with visa conditions may result in fines or detention.
Mexico allows travellers to enter the country with a duty-free personal allowance (franquicia) up to a specified value.
If the value of goods you're bringing into Mexico exceeds the permitted allowance, you'll be required to pay the applicable customs duties and taxes on the excess amount.
Before you travel, make sure you understand Mexico's customs allowance limits and keep receipts or proof of purchase for high-value items.
Customs officials may ask you to demonstrate the value of goods you're carrying, including electronics, gifts, luxury items, and other newly purchased products.
If you're travelling with goods that exceed the duty-free allowance, be prepared to declare them and pay any applicable taxes on arrival.
Failure to declare goods may result in delays, fines, confiscation of items, or further action by Mexican customs authorities.
Customs procedures can vary between airports and border crossings.
If you're unsure whether an item must be declared, ask a customs official before passing through the customs control area.
For information on current allowance limits and duty payments, consult Mexican customs authorities before departure.

A child under 18 years who's also a citizen or resident of Mexico must carry a  Mexican Minor Travel Consent Form  (Spanish) or a  notarised consent  if travelling with anyone other than their parent or legal guardian.
You may need a permit if you arrive in Mexico by motor vehicle.

Advice for people travelling with children
Many countries require your passport to be valid for at least 6 months before entering or departing.
This includes for transit.
If your passport expires too soon, you might not be able to travel.
The Australian Government doesn’t make these rules.
Check your passport’s expiry date before you go.
If you’re not sure, it’s a good idea to  get a new passport .
Lost or stolen passport
Your passport is valuable and can be used by others to commit crimes.
Don’t let anyone trick you into giving it to them.
Always keep it in a safe place.
If your passport is lost or stolen, tell the Australian Government:
In Australia: contact the  Australian Passport Information Service .
Overseas: contact the nearest  Australian embassy or consulate .
Passport with 'X' gender identifier
Australian passports comply with international standards for sex and gender.
If your passport has an 'X' in the sex field, we can’t guarantee your entry or transit to other countries.
Ask the  embassy or consulate of your destinations if they’ll accept your passport with an 'X' gender marker.
Check all transit destinations too.
Mexico's official currency is the Mexican Peso (MXN).
Declare amounts over USD$10,000 or foreign currency equivalent.
Do this on arrival and departure.
This covers all forms of currency, not only cash.
US dollars are widely accepted in holiday resort areas.
You can't generally exchange Australian currency and traveller's cheques in Mexico.
ATMs are widely available in cities and towns.
Take care as credit card fraud occurs.
Carry cash if you're travelling to rural areas.
Most international hotels and tourist facilities accept credit and debit cards.
Ask your bank whether your ATM card will work in Mexico.
Scams that affect travellers
Plan your travel carefully.
Some cities should only be accessed directly by air travel or major toll roads wherever possible.
Other travel options and routes may have higher security risks.
Our consular partners (Canada, United Kingdom and United States) also publish travel advisories for Mexico that you can read.
Transport and adventure tour operators may not meet Australian safety standards for:
scuba diving equipment
providing life jackets, seat belts and other safety equipment
Rescue services may not be as effective as those in Australia.
If taking part in adventure activities:
check your travel insurance covers you for the activity
check the operator's safety equipment before you book
use reputable operators
use life jackets and seat belts, even if others don't
If appropriate safety equipment isn't available, use another provider.
To drive a vehicle, you'll need a current Australian driver's licence.
Foreign digital driver’s licences are not accepted.
Vehicles generally don't stop for pedestrians or indicate when they're turning.
Intersections can be confusing, with vehicles coming from unexpected directions.
Strict laws cover insurance and reporting of accidents.
If you choose to drive:
learn local road use and driving rules
keep doors locked and windows up, even when moving
only travel during daylight hours and allow enough time to get to your destination before dark
use toll roads (cuota) to reduce the risk of crime
If you're a victim of roadside robbery or stopped at a roadblock, do as you're asked.
Driving on rural roads is dangerous due to:
poor road conditions.
pedestrians and livestock on roads
inadequate street lighting and signage
Criminals target vehicles, including campervans and SUVs, especially in rural areas.
Use ride-share services, where possible, instead of taxis.
If this isn't an option, it's best to use registered official taxis and limousines, preferably arranged through your hotel.
use official taxis from airports
pre-pay your fare at an official taxi company booth at the airport
use official taxis or taxis waiting at assigned stands (sitios), especially in Mexico City
Crime levels on intercity buses and highways are high, and the risks increase after dark.
Use first-class buses.
Women travelling on public transport should be cautious.
Transport and getting around safely
DFAT doesn’t give advice about how safe different airlines or flight paths are.
Safety risks and weather events may cause flight delays and cancellations.
Contact your travel agent or airline to confirm your plans.
Airlines and airports can restrict what you can take in your luggage.
Regulations may be different to those in Australia.
Check with your airline before you travel.
